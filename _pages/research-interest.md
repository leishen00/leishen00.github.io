My research focuses on the molecular mechanisms, biological characteristics, and potential therapeutic targets of gliomas. By integrating transcriptomics, proteomics, and single-cell transcriptomics, I aim to dissect the complexity of gliomas and identify novel biomarkers and therapeutic targets. Equally important, I seek to validate these omics-derived findings through comprehensive cellular, molecular, and animal experiments, thereby uncovering deeper molecular mechanisms. I am proficient in using R, Python, and other computational tools to analyze high-throughput omics data—including transcriptomics, proteomics, and single-cell RNA sequencing—to identify key genes, signaling pathways, and tumor microenvironmental features.

## Research Interest 1

I aim to explore the molecular heterogeneity of lower-grade gliomas (LGG) through multi-omics integration. LGGs, typically defined as WHO grade II–III diffuse gliomas, generally display less malignancy than glioblastoma (GBM). However, a subset of patients still undergo malignant progression and ultimately develop GBM. Current classification systems insufficiently capture LGG heterogeneity. While GBM has established molecular subtypes at both the bulk and single-cell RNA-seq levels, LGG classification still largely relies on histological origin and certain molecular features (e.g., IDH status, 1p/19q co-deletion), lacking broadly accepted transcriptomic subtypes. In my preliminary work, I utilized TCGA and CGGA bulk RNA-seq data, together with publicly available LGG single-cell datasets from GEO, and identified distinct molecular features warranting further study. In the future, I intend to integrate bulk and single-cell datasets to characterize LGG heterogeneity across multiple dimensions, and ultimately propose a clinically meaningful classification system that could help predict LGG progression to GBM, thereby optimizing treatment strategies and patient follow-up.

## Research Interest 2

I seek to investigate the molecular mechanisms underlying GBM initiation and progression, with the goal of providing novel perspectives for clinical diagnosis and prognostic evaluation. GBM is the most common and deadliest primary malignant tumor of the central nervous system in adults, characterized by marked heterogeneity, invasiveness, and therapeutic resistance. Although previous studies have identified mutations and pathway alterations involving IDH, TP53, EGFR, PTEN, and others, a systematic understanding of its dynamic evolutionary trajectory remains limited. I plan to employ single-cell and spatial transcriptomics as core technologies, complemented by proteomics and epigenomics, to chart cellular state transitions and microenvironmental remodeling during GBM initiation, progression, metastasis, and treatment response. By dissecting key functional cell populations (e.g., tumor stem cells, immunosuppressive cells) and their communication networks, I aim to pinpoint critical drivers of malignant progression. Ultimately, my goal is to identify clinically predictive biomarkers that can support precision diagnosis and prognostic assessment.

## Research Interest 3

I aspire to develop novel therapeutic strategies for GBM by leveraging interdisciplinary approaches that combine biomedical and engineering sciences. Given GBM’s invasiveness, resistance to therapy, and the restrictive blood–brain barrier, current treatment modalities face significant limitations. I plan to collaborate with materials science and engineering fields to design targeted and biocompatible nanomedicine delivery systems that enhance drug accumulation in tumor regions. Furthermore, by exploiting tumor-specific microenvironmental features, I intend to investigate photo- or magneto-responsive therapeutic systems, including photothermal/photodynamic therapy and acoustic/electrical stimulation, and comprehensively evaluate their safety and efficacy in vivo. Additionally, I will incorporate multimodal imaging and tissue engineering models to more precisely assess interactions between GBM cells and their microenvironment (e.g., immune cells, vasculature, and glial cells), thereby providing experimental support for personalized therapies. By integrating biomedical research with engineering innovations, I aim to facilitate translation from basic research to clinical application, ultimately driving breakthroughs in GBM treatment paradigms.
